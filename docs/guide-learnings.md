# Guide learning archive (pred.gg)

Every explicit lesson from the pred.gg community guide catalog for patches
**1.16, 1.15, and 1.14** (all 181 guides in those families), plus the
most-viewed guides of every earlier patch family back to the 0.16 era.
Compiled 2026-09-08. Companion to `docs/player-guides-digest.md` (the
thematic digest); this file is the per-guide record — one entry per guide,
carrying only what its author explicitly teaches.

**Reading rules.**
- An entry's bullets are the guide's own claims, kept faithful (quotes are
  verbatim). They are SOURCED community knowledge, not verified fact:
  item names, numbers and thresholds go stale with every patch, and only
  entries at the current patch describe the live game.
- "Build template only — no explicit lessons" is an honest verdict, not a
  gap: roughly seven in ten published guides carry no explanatory prose.
  The teaching concentrates in a small set of authors.
- For the coach: concepts and vocabulary from here may frame a review;
  any NUMBER in a review still comes only from that game's facts file
  (honesty contract, `docs/coaching-methodology.md`).

**Coverage.** 190 entries: 53 in the 1.16 family, 81 in 1.15, 48 in
1.14 (every guide listed on pred.gg for those families at compile time,
minus two junk/test entries), plus 8 most-viewed picks from 1.13 back to
0.16. 64 carry explicit lessons; 126 are build templates.

## Cross-cutting lessons (what the catalog teaches as a whole)

1. **The swap grammar is the lingua franca.** The single most common form
   of guide teaching, across every author and even across languages
   ("los 2 últimos los cambias dependiendo el otro equipo"), is
   *{default} → {swap} when {condition}*. The canonical conditions:
   cleanse item vs heavy CC; anti-heal (Tainted line) vs sustain;
   Megacosm vs health-stackers; penetration vs armor-stackers; last one
   or two slots flexed to the enemy comp's damage profile.
2. **Defensive choices are counted, not felt.** The better guides make the
   CC condition numeric: a tenacity build "when the enemy team has lots of
   stuns to interrupt your ultimate — against one or fewer enemy stuns use
   higher damage"; Unbroken Will "if the enemy has 1+ CC abilities (stuns,
   roots, tethers, airborne)". Count the enemy kit, then itemize.
3. **Hold-and-upgrade slot economy.** A recurring build-order idiom: buy a
   cheap stacking component early ("Buy Soul Chalice 2nd and hold"),
   let it stack all game, and complete its upgrade as the LAST item — or
   early "if you need the shield to survive burst". Item slots are also
   liquid late: sell the early mana trinket, sell the gold-engine item at
   full build, replace utility with survivability once cooldowns stop
   mattering.
4. **Name the engine.** The most-read builds are organized around one
   scaling engine stated in a sentence: high base health feeding World
   Breaker; burn damage charging healing procs ("use the burning from
   Brimstone to charge Lunaria and Lifecore"); a haste rush feeding an
   Eternal's gold generation; one payoff item the whole build exists to
   enable. Everything else in the build feeds it.
5. **Don't build away from the kit.** Explicit warnings against stat
   builds that abandon a hero's mechanism: an attack build on an
   ability hero means "losing all her skills that she's so powerful
   with"; a proc item is chosen because the kit's 2.5s cooldown feeds it.
   The kit's cast rate and mechanics pick the items.
6. **Combos are taught as sequences with an exit.** Real combo teaching
   names the order AND the way out ("reactivate Shadowslip to exit"),
   weaves basics between casts, and exploits reset mechanics ("Blink
   refreshes every time you hit Psychic Flare — abuse it to engage or to
   rat out of ganks").
7. **Mechanic fine print decides fights.** The catalog carries load-bearing
   fine print players quote: slow stacking is not additive (strongest +5%
   per extra source); anti-heal doesn't stack but dual anti-heal is right
   when one vector can't apply; an ult that "does not grant CC immunity —
   don't use it recklessly"; what a reflect CANNOT bounce; teleporting
   cancels a channeled ult.
8. **Early safety is item-gated.** "Play more safe until the first 2 items
   are online unless you are really good with conserving mana" — the early
   game is graded against item count and mana discipline, not bravado.
9. **The best coaching line in the catalog** (The Wraith Codex): "Mastery
   is not achieved by winning more games than everyone else. It is
   achieved by making better decisions than you made yesterday." Its
   philosophy — "Gold before Glory": every completed item is a bigger
   spike than any outplay.


## Patch 1.16 family

### Hybrid Builds with every Eternal — Boris, Jungle (v1.16.4, 1258 views, by Peter Weeber) `u85Tf0K`
- Hybrid core (Typhoon crest, Prophecy, Plasma Blade, Cursed Ring) exists to hit the 460% attack-speed cap: "Magic Power passively provides attack speed and will feed into Cursed Ring to reach 460% once it's obtained."
- Sequencing lesson: buy Physical Power components first so the Magic Power rush doesn't leave the early game weak.
- One build per Eternal, tiered beginner→master by execution cost; Exarch is the beginner entry ("fastest jungle in the game", first clear 2:25).
- Nihil (mana burn) is declared dead against manaless heroes: Akeron, Eden, Khaimera, Scarlett.
- Xyris is CS-gated: "need to farm 150 CS to proc Bone Collector… be unstoppable after 30+ minutes" — weak until, unstoppable after.
- Always take the Chrono Swipe augment because "Chrono Swipe counts as basic attacks which works with a plethora of on-hit elements."
- Universal last-slot flex: Unbroken Will / Legacy / Salvation "if against heavy cc comp."

### Hybrid Builds with every Eternal — Grux, Jungle (v1.16.4, 160 views, by Peter Weeber) `kw1Z9Os`
- The hybrid case is patch-historical: 1.14.4 made Magic Power boost both Bloodlust damage and Warlord's Challenge damage steal — builds follow patch mechanics, not tradition.
- Augment lock-in with a stated reason: Lust For Blood because Red Mist's 40% attack speed is redundant once items push past the 420% cap — never take a stat you already cap.
- Per-Eternal playstyle shifts are explicit: Marrow = "play aggressive when hurt"; Thraex = hunt isolated targets (+14% vs isolated); Lotus = stack movespeed and chase; Vesh = chain kills to refresh the ultimate; Krix = "build health defensively; convert it offensively."
- Kallari-era warning generalized: an Eternal that removes a mobility perk can be a downgrade even when its damage is higher.

### Iggy & Scorch jungle is real! — Iggy & Scorch, Jungle (v1.16.4, 100 views, by Peter Weeber) `76kjbkd`
- The whole game is scheduled around objective respawns: Fangtooth 5:00/10:00/15:00 then Primal at 20:00; Mini Orb 7:00/13:00/19:00; camps ~60s.
- Terror blessing is the enabling piece: at level 5 he can "walk away from Fangtooth tanking it with 60–75% health remaining" — solo objectives come from one loadout choice.
- Fangtooth stacks: 2% team damage per kill, "8% permanent" at the third (~15:00) — "your number #1 priority no exceptions"; with Growing Threat = ~20% at 16 minutes.
- Turret-charge budgeting formula: "0x charges at 3:40 = STOP USING FLAME TURRET UNTIL 5:00" — spend cooldowns against the objective clock.
- Gank philosophy: early ganks forbidden ("this entire gameplan bursts into flames if you die early"); ganks are a byproduct of map control.
- Mini Orb conversion: "Orb buff and 4x turrets MELTS plated towers in seconds… it is fine if you die with mini orb buff so long as you make enemy react and or destroy tower(s)" — the Giga Push.
- Deward before every objective; buy a Sentry on the ~0:38 first recall.
